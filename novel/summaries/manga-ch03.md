# Previously: manga chapter 3, "Passenger Seat"

A week later Kubo hands Dai his first pay envelope: "Don't spend it all on gas." "I don't have a car." "Yet." Two weeks of nights make a thin stack of bills. Dai walks home at dawn and leaves half on the shoe cabinet, where his mother once left Sota's lunch money. Michiko finds the money. Aya admits she thought he was in trouble; "So did I," says Michiko, and she kneels to pull a blanket over him while Sota watches, smiling.

Thursday. The red Roadster arrives on a dry, clear night. Natsume remembers his name and tells him to stop staring at her car like it owes him money. Then she asks when his break is. Kubo waves him off, and she opens the passenger door: "One lap. Then you'll understand why you should stay off that road."

She drives the harbor road and onto the bypass at full speed. Dai watches her hands and feet: heel-and-toe on every downshift. They go under the overpass where he once stood ("That night I was up there. Now I'm down here"), and when she glances over expecting fear, he is laughing.

On the empty pier lot she says, "You were supposed to be scared." "I was. But I was watching your feet." Nobody, she says, sees that from the passenger seat. Asked why he wants it so badly, he says nobody has ever looked at him and thought "wow." She drives him back and tells Kubo: "He's got no sense. But he's got good eyes."

Alone, she looks at an old photo tucked in her sun visor: her younger self, laughing, beside a young man and a car, his face out of focus. At dawn Dai sits on his futon working an invisible gear lever. Sota asks what he's doing. "...Practicing."

## State at end
- First pay 39,200 yen; half given home. Michiko and Aya now trust the job.
- Dai has ridden once with Natsume and seen heel-and-toe; she now takes him seriously.
- Natsume tried to scare him off the road and failed.
- The visor photo is known only to the reader.
- Dai still has no car and no license.
