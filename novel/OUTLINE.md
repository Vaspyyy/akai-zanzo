## 0. For the creator: the big choices to look at first

All eight are PROVISIONAL until you approve or replace them.

1. **Akane's partner.** Kenji Sakuma, then 23, crashed on the bypass curve under Dai's overpass on the night of Thursday 14 October 1993. His black Toyota MR2 snapped sideways when he lifted off mid-corner trying to keep up with Akane, and hit the foot of the overpass stairs. **He survived, paralyzed from the waist down.** He told Akane to leave before the police came and told them he was alone, to protect her place in nursing school. The scene's rumor is "she left him there." (Alternative: he died. I chose survival because a living, sharp, funny Sakuma gives us scenes, keeps the cost visible, and makes the romance honestly complicated.)
2. **Akane's life.** Born 3 Nov 1973. A newly qualified nurse (since April 1995) on night shifts in the emergency room of Shiomi Municipal Hospital. Thursday is the night she visits Sakuma and then runs the bypass alone. The red Roadster was Sakuma's first car: he taught her to drive in it and sold it to her for one yen in 1992. She keeps it exactly as he set it up. The visor photo shows the two of them with this same car.
3. **Dai's calendar.** Born **9 February 1978**. He is in his final year of high school (graduates 1 March 1996). The canon "last three months before 18" run from November 1995 to 9 February 1996 (Volume 2). Book I runs from 25 August 1995 to March 1997.
4. **Dai's car** (within the canon scrapyard Roadster). A 1989 Eunos Roadster in faded Mariner Blue from Fumie Tanabe's scrapyard: rear-ended, timing belt snapped, roof torn, mismatched replacement panels. 60,000 yen plus Sunday labor. It stays registered in Fumie's name because he is a minor. The scene calls it **"Tsugihagi"** ("patchwork") as an insult, and the name slowly turns into respect.
5. **The rival** (bible §11). Sei Kirishima, 22, son of the big local Honda dealer, in a brand-new Integra Type R (on sale October 1995). Polite, honest, trained on circuits, heading for pro racing (a Book II seed). His question mirrors Dai's: is he good, or just well equipped?
6. **Betting and police.** Saturday meets at the Pier 3 lot, one-on-one runs on "the Loop", a tire-shop owner (Okabe) who holds the stakes and keeps 10%. The money is in underdog odds, so Dai's growing name shrinks his income. A polite loan shark (Sawada) waits at the edge. Sergeant Mizuno of the traffic police has hunted the 1993 "second car" for two years and believes he has only until mid-October 1996 (a three-year limitation; verify the law, the story only needs the clock).
7. **The family.** Dai's father, Masaru, was a gambler who left in 1987 with loan-shark debts. That is why Michiko fears the betting more than the driving. Dai accepts a factory job offer (Sanko Brake) to give her hope, and loses it in Volume 5.
8. **The big costs and the ending** (bible §11). In Volume 5 Dai spends the money for Sota's home nebulizer on tires and Sota lands in hospital, and in the same weeks Goto (unlicensed, in debt) crashes into spectators while racing Dai. Nobu is badly hurt. Goto tells the police he was alone, exactly as Sakuma once did, and goes to prison. Book I ends with a race for no money and almost no audience (Dai vs Kirishima), Akane in Dai's passenger seat for the first time, and a stranger's kid on the overpass watching red taillights.

## 1. Book I at a glance

- **Length:** 7 volumes, 78 chapters, about 470,000 words. Chapters 5,000-7,000 words; volumes 60,000-80,000.
- **Span:** Friday 25 August 1995 (the morning after manga ch3) to March 1997. Dai goes from 17 to 19.
- **Book I question** (bible §11): *If nobody watched you race, would you still drive?* Dai starts out wanting to be looked at ("wow"). He ends knowing why he drives.
- **Shape:** V1 wanting. V2 breaking rules. V3 being allowed. V4 being known. V5 paying. V6 putting debts down. V7 driving for its own sake.
- **Races:** about ten real races in the whole book. Most chapters have none. Lessons, practice and night drives carry the driving in between.
- **Romance [CANON constraint]:** nothing romantic before Dai turns 18. After that, slow. First kiss not before Volume 6, and it complicates more than it solves.
- **Book II boundary [CANON]:** pro racing is only talked about (Kirishima's plans, his card at the end). No circuits in Book I.
- **POV rule:** the novel shows only what Dai sees, hears or is told. (The manga may add scenes without him, like the visor panel in ch3.)

## 2. Novel to manga mapping

One novel chapter becomes one manga chapter of 25-30 pages by default. Chapters with a race or two big set pieces split into two. The manga may cut interior monologue and turn quiet passages into silent panels, but never adds plot that contradicts the novel.

## 3. World additions

- **The Loop** (about 7 km, about 4 minutes): start at the Pier 3 lot → the warehouse straight → the freight-line rail crossing (a bump that unsettles cars; a rare night freight) → the S-bend between the cement silos → the bypass on-ramp → the long left-hander under the pedestrian overpass (Dai's spot, and the 1993 crash site) → off at the Higashi exit → back down to the harbor.
- **Meets:** Saturdays after 1 a.m. at Pier 3; scooter lookouts with walkie-talkies at the on-ramp. On Thursdays the lot is empty (manga ch3).
- **Money:** Okabe holds stakes in a cash box, writes everything in a school notebook, keeps 10%. Side bets in the crowd; odds follow reputation. Usual stakes 10,000-100,000 yen.
- **Police:** Shiomi Police traffic division; white-and-black Crown patrol cars; checkpoints in the year-end safety campaign; raids that close the harbor road. Racing together is "joint dangerous driving" (license revoked). If Dai is caught **unlicensed before 18**, he can't get a license for about a year (verify) and, with his facility record, goes to family court.
- **Minors:** adulthood is 20 [period fact]. Dai needs a parent's consent for contracts: car registration, and (verify) driving-school enrollment. Hence the car in Fumie's name and the forged family hanko.
- **Places:** Tanabe Auto Dismantling (past the cement silos; lessons in its back lot); Shiomi Municipal Hospital; Okabe Tire; Kawamata Ramen; Sanko Brake's Shiomi plant; Sakuma's ground-floor apartment in the old town.
- **Off the books:** Kubo pays Dai a flat 700 yen cash, no paperwork, no night premium. Keeping a minor on fixed nights like this breaks labor law, and it costs Kubo his job in Volume 4.

## 4. Volume plans

Each table shows where things stand at the **start → end** of the volume.

### Volume 1: Good Eyes
*25 Aug-14 Nov 1995. 12 chapters. Manga ch4-17. Full chapter plan in section 7.*

Dai has had one lap in the passenger seat and wants everything that comes after it. Natsume won't teach him. School pushes a factory job, and his mother's hope makes him say yes to the exam. At the Pier 3 meet he sees the scene's economy and its risk in one night. A scrapyard sells him a dead Roadster for money he doesn't have. Sota's asthma attack puts Dai in the emergency room, where the nurse is Natsume, and seeing his family changes what she does next: she teaches him on private ground, under strict rules. He stalls eleven times. Kubo brings the engine back. Dai wins his first small bet on someone else's race. He finds the wrecked black MR2 under Fumie's tarp, and Akane tells him one true sentence about the pillar under the overpass. The car runs. He promised to stay on private ground, and the volume ends with him driving out of the gate.

**Question:** Is wanting enough?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | no car, no license, one ride → owns a running wreck, has driven on the public road once, illegally |
| Akane | "stay off that road" → teaches him in the yard; has told him one sentence of her past |
| Family | Mom's first hope → a job offer on the fridge; nobody knows about the car |
| Money | ~18,900 yen → broke: car part-paid, parts bought, a small bet win spent |
| Car | none → the 1989 Roadster runs |
| Reputation | none → Goto has seen "the bridge kid" in a patchwork car |

**Set pieces:** the Pier 3 meet and the police scatter; the ER, where the nurse is Natsume; eleven stalls in the red Roadster; the engine catching.
**Hook:** he breaks his promise, and Goto sees him on the harbor road.

### Volume 2: Three Months
*14 Nov 1995-9 Feb 1996. 12 chapters. The canon unlicensed months.*

Dai drives at night behind Akane's back. Goto spreads the joke: a kid in a patchwork Roadster who drives like a driving-school brochure. Dai needs money (the rest of Fumie's price, tires, driving school, the shaken due in March), so he bets on himself for the first time: 20,000 yen against Goto's sidekick Tetsu. He loses. He brakes where Akane brakes, but his car isn't hers (tired shocks, no limited-slip), and he spins harmlessly at the silo S-bend. Two months of savings, gone. He learns his own car and then wins twice at long odds, once by reading the rail-crossing bump and once in the rain. At a Sunday lesson Akane reads the wear on his tires and knows he lied; she ends the lessons. December's safety campaign brings a checkpoint. Dai escapes with his lights off through the container yard, and Sergeant Mizuno visits Fumie to ask who drives the Roadster registered to her. Fumie lies. New Year at home is the warm center: ozōni, Sota's otoshidama, Aya bringing her boyfriend Morita to a cramped, comic dinner. In late January Goto challenges Dai for real money, and mid-race the police close the harbor road. Nobody wins. Goto is caught at the rail crossing (license revoked, Silvia held as evidence); Tetsu hits a barricade and ends up in Akane's ER. Dai gets away and knows it was luck. He turns 18 at midnight during his Thursday shift; at 2 a.m. Akane pulls in, says "Happy birthday, kid," and leaves cheap new driving gloves on the pump (a truce, not a romance). At 4 a.m. he stamps his mother's hanko on the driving-school consent form.

**Question:** What will he break to get there?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | 17, driving illegally → 18, enrolled at driving school on a forged consent |
| Akane | teacher → betrayed, lessons ended → birthday truce |
| Family | in the dark → still in the dark; Aya suspects |
| Money | broke → ~310,000 yen in a cookie tin taped under the clothes chest |
| Car | barely running → road-sorted: Okabe's used shocks, new pads, a junkyard seat |
| Reputation | nobody → "Tsugihagi", a joke that sometimes wins |

**Set pieces:** the lost first bet; the lights-off escape; New Year dinner; the raid.
**Hook:** the forged hanko.

### Volume 3: Eighteen
*10 Feb-30 Apr 1996. 11 chapters.*

Dai gets what he wanted, and it's boring. Driving school humbles him: instructor Ōnishi, an ex-traffic cop, fails his first check for skipped safety looks and a crank course taken like a racer, and Dai has to learn to drive slowly and politely. Graduation nearly doesn't happen (attendance); Mr. Hotta bends a rule, and Michiko comes in her one good suit. He passes the license test in late March and spends 100,000 yen on the Roadster's shaken, still in Fumie's name. On 1 April he starts on the day line at Sanko Brake and keeps two nights at Kubo's; he is always tired. Akane takes him back on her terms now that he is legal, and teaches him the bypass curve, leading in her car while he follows, mirror to mirror. Kirishima challenges him properly and beats him by a long way on the Loop; Dai runs clean and earns a can of coffee and a real conversation. Then Goto, carless and bitter, tells Dai the rumor: the Akai Zanzō left her partner dying under the overpass. Dai throws it at Akane, badly. She answers by taking him, on a Thursday, to an apartment in the old town. Kenji Sakuma is alive, in a wheelchair, funny and sharp. "She didn't leave me. I told her to go." He shows Dai the original of the visor photo: the red Roadster, new, with the two of them.

**Question:** Now that he's allowed, is he any good?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | unlicensed → licensed factory worker, fairly beaten by Kirishima |
| Akane | wary truce → lets him into her past; "Call me Akane" (narration switches from "Natsume") |
| Family | hope → a proud graduation, the job starts; secrets intact |
| Money | ~310,000 → ~60,000 after school, shaken, insurance; factory pay ~150,000/month, half home |
| Car | unregistered to him, unlicensed driver → legal, still patchwork |
| Reputation | joke → "the kid who ran clean against Kirishima" |

**Set pieces:** the driving-school crank course; graduation; the first legal night run behind Akane; the Kirishima race; Sakuma's door.
**Hook:** Sakuma mentions a cop who has spent two and a half years looking for a second car, and Dai realizes it's the cop who came to Fumie's yard.

### Volume 4: Odds
*May-July 1996. 11 chapters.*

Dai wins. In the rainy season the Roadster's balance beats horsepower and he takes four runs in six weeks; at Pier 3 people say "Tsugihagi" without laughing. But the shorter his odds, the less he earns: the money only ever came from being underestimated. Sawada offers 300,000 yen to lose one run. Dai says no and keeps thinking about it. At the stamping press he nods off and nearly loses a hand. Nobu's photo of the patchwork Roadster runs on the reader page of *Street Tune* magazine; Mizuno sees it and comes to the gas station, and the owner learns about last year's off-the-books minor. Kubo is fired. On 1 June, the seasonal clothes change, Michiko finds the tin (420,000 yen), Okabe's chits and the driving-school receipt with her own seal on it. "Your father kept his slips in a drawer too." Dai leaves and sleeps in Fumie's site office. Aya brings his clothes and takes 100,000 yen for her beauty-school correspondence course, hating herself a little. Akane comes to the yard at night more than she needs to; once Dai nearly says something and she shuts it down. Kirishima turns into a friend, which makes Dai worse company. In July, a rematch in the rain: Dai beats Kirishima by a car length.

**Question:** What is a name worth if it costs you your home?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | factory worker at home → winning, sleeping in a scrapyard office |
| Akane | teacher → something neither of them names |
| Family | secrets → Mom knows everything; Aya caught between |
| Money | ~60,000 → peaks at 420,000, ends ~250,000 |
| Car | stock → better shocks, a limited-slip diff from a wrecked 1.8 Roadster |
| Reputation | "the kid" → "Tsugihagi", said with respect; beat Kirishima once |

**Set pieces:** the rainy-season run; Michiko and the tin; the rain rematch.
**Hook:** Goto turns up at the yard, still unlicensed, his Silvia back from police storage and rebuilt on money borrowed from Sawada: "You owe me a finish."

### Volume 5: Gallery
*Aug-Sept 1996. 11 chapters. ("Gallery" is what the scene calls its spectators.)*

One year since the overpass. Michiko comes to the yard with a bento and says the only thing she wants is for him to come home alive. He goes home for Obon, and the family watches the harbor fireworks from the danchi roof: the last easy night. Sota's doctor says a home nebulizer (38,000 yen) would keep him out of the ER, and Dai promises one. Goto's "finish" becomes the biggest run Pier 3 has seen: half a million yen, most of it borrowed from Sawada. Dai's tires are worn to the cords and the summer heat kills grip; he spends the nebulizer money on new ones. That week Sota has a night attack while Dai is out practicing. Akane is on duty when Sota is admitted, and tells Dai what he did in four plain sentences. The race: the Loop lined with gallery. Goto, desperate, takes the silo S-bend too fast and the Silvia goes into the crowd. Nobu's leg is shattered, three others are hurt, Goto is trapped. Dai stops, runs back, and does what Akane taught him (keep Nobu still, keep him talking) until the ambulances come. Goto tells the police he was alone. Mizuno, interviewing Dai: "That's the second time someone has told me that." Pier 3 is shut down. Sanko Brake lets Dai go after days of absence and a police phone call.

**Question:** Who pays for his races?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | winning, proud → jobless, untouched by the law, guilty |
| Akane | close → furious, then the one who sits with him at 4 a.m. |
| Family | tentative peace → Sota in hospital; Michiko's worst night |
| Money | ~250,000 → nearly gone: tires, Sota's bill, Nobu's family |
| Car | fine, new tires → fine (that's the obscene part) |
| Reputation | respected → known as "the kid in Goto's crash" |

**Set pieces:** fireworks from the danchi roof; Sota's admission; the crash into the gallery.
**Hook:** Akane: "Now you know what I've known for three years." Mizuno's deadline is weeks away.

### Volume 6: Three Years
*Oct-Dec 1996. 10 chapters.*

Putting debts down. Nobu is angry, then quiet, then working: rehab, a limp, a notebook. Goto pleads guilty and goes to prison; in the visiting room he tells Dai, "Don't you dare. I hit them. Not you." Kubo opens a two-bay garage under the railway arches with his last savings, and Dai works there for real wages. With days left on the limitation, Mizuno visits Akane at the hospital: the 1993 caller who phoned 119 said "don't move his neck," like a nursing student. Akane doesn't go to the police first. She goes to Sakuma's mother and tells her the truth about that night, and only after the deadline has passed tells Mizuno too. He listens, writes nothing, and buys her coffee. Sakuma wants to drive again. Dai and Kubo fit hand controls to a cheap automatic Corolla, and Sakuma drives the bypass curve at the speed limit for the first time since 1993, with Dai in the passenger seat. He lets Fumie crush the MR2 and gives Dai its Momo steering wheel. Dai finds his father in a pachinko parlor two towns over and doesn't speak to him: the "guy nobody remembers" from manga ch1, in person. In December, on the pier, Akane and Dai kiss once, and then she tells him she has been offered ICU training at a Tokyo hospital from April.

**Question:** Can you put down what you owe?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | guilty, jobless → working at Kubo's, paying Nobu's family monthly |
| Akane | bound by the secret → free of it; maybe leaving |
| Family | wounded → rebuilding; Dai sleeps at home again |
| Money | near zero → a small honest wage |
| Car | as was → Sakuma's steering wheel |
| Reputation | the scene is dark; his name is a rumor |

**Set pieces:** Akane and Sakuma's mother; Sakuma's drive; the pachinko parlor; the pier.
**Hook:** Kirishima, leaving for a racing school in March, asks for one last run: no money, no gallery, "just to know."

### Volume 7: Afterimage
*Jan-Mar 1997. 11 chapters.*

Winter after the crackdown. Pier 3 is dark. There is nothing to win and nobody to watch, which is the book's question in plain form. Fumie sells the yard to the port's new logistics company; the gate closes at the end of March. Goto's letters from prison are short and badly spelled. Aya marries Morita in a rented hall, and Dai sees his whole family dressed up and laughing at once. He turns 19. He prepares for Kirishima without bets: Kubo's work, Sakuma's wheel, the same patchwork body. The run is at night on the Loop, watched by Akane, Sakuma, Kubo, Fumie, Nobu on crutches, and one kid on the overpass nobody knows. Dai wins by half a car length through the long curve. Kirishima tells the scene "He beat me," which is the only way anyone hears of it. Mizuno arrives afterwards, looks at both cars, and writes Dai a ticket for a broken taillight. Then Akane gets into Dai's passenger seat for the first time, and they drive the harbor road at the limit (the legal one). The book ends on the kid on the overpass, watching taillights go, and Dai understanding that the afterimage was never about the car's color: all taillights are red.

**Question:** If nobody watched you race, would you still drive?

| | Start → End |
| --- | --- |
| Dai | 18, nothing to race for → 19, knows why he drives; respected by the few who matter |
| Akane | deciding → leaves for Tokyo in April with a promise neither of them says out loud |
| Family | rebuilding → changed: Aya married, Sota better, Michiko tired but laughing |
| Money | a wage → a wage, and an honest envelope for Mom |
| Car | patchwork → still patchwork (his Book II attachment, bible §11) |
| Reputation | rumor → earned by testimony, not spectacle |

**Set pieces:** the yard's last night; Aya's wedding; the final run; the passenger seat.
**Hook into Book II:** Kirishima's business card in the glovebox.

## 7. Volume 1 chapter by chapter

Dates are 1995. Dai works Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday nights (22:00-06:00) and is paid every other Wednesday (see LEDGER).

**Ch1. Practicing** (25 Aug-1 Sep; manga ch4). Picks up at dawn where manga ch3 ends: Dai shifting an invisible gear lever, Sota watching. He sleeps two hours and goes to the last day of summer remedial classes, where Nobu Kawamata (failed English, same as Dai) spots the pedal diagrams in his notebook and won't stop talking about Roadsters; when Dai says he rode in the Akai Zanzō last night, Nobu doesn't believe a word. Dai reads a driving-technique book standing up in a bookstore until the owner shoos him out with a feather duster, then does the math on the back of a receipt: a license costs 280,000 yen, six months of his half-pay if he never buys anything. Thursday 31 Aug: he's at the station early, and she doesn't come. Kubo: "She's not a train." Walking home over the overpass at dawn, he notices a section of guardrail newer than the rest and a scrubbed-pale scrape at the foot of the stairs. Ends: "She didn't come." The hunger with no object; the crash site planted. (Sample scene in STYLE.md.)

**Ch2. Second Term** (1-8 Sep; manga ch5). School restarts, and Mr. Hotta hands the third-years their job-placement sheets: Sanko Brake's Shiomi plant wants line workers, 148,000 yen a month, bonuses, a dormitory. Hotta has already phoned Michiko. That night there is karaage for dinner, bought with money she doesn't have, and her hope is so plain that Dai agrees to sit the exam on 19 Sep; Aya gives him a week. Payday, half on the shoe cabinet again. Thursday 7 Sep, 2:05 a.m.: the red Roadster is back. Natsume says she had "a shift" last week. Dai asks her to teach him. "I don't teach." When he won't step back from the window: "Get a license. Then we'll see." Ends with Dai alone at the pump saying the number out loud: 280,000 yen. Two futures side by side, both offered by women who want him safe.

**Ch3. Pier 3** (Sat 9-Sun 10 Sep; manga ch6-7). Nobu has read that Shiomi has a scene; Dai knows where it meets. Nobu tells his father he's studying at Dai's. After 1 a.m. the Pier 3 lot holds fifty people: Eurobeat from car stereos, cigarettes, girls sitting on hoods, and a man with a cash box and a school notebook (Okabe) writing down bets. Goto recognizes "the bridge kid" and asks if he's still chasing afterimages. Dai watches Goto beat a Toyota Chaser on the Loop and watches the money more closely than the race: who pays, who gets paid, how the odds move when the crowd decides who's going to lose. Okabe tells a driver, "You can always bet on yourself," and Dai hears it. Then a lookout's walkie-talkie crackles: patrol car at the on-ramp. Everyone scatters. Dai and Nobu hide in a warehouse doorway (Nobu loses a sandal) while a patrol car's spotlight sweeps the lot and an older officer gets out, picks up a dropped betting chit, and looks for a long time at the shadows where they are. Ends at 4 a.m. on the walk home: a way to make money, and no car to make it with.

**Ch4. Tanabe Auto Dismantling** (11-17 Sep; manga ch8). The used-car papers say a Roadster costs 700,000 yen and up. Kubo, without looking up: "If you want a car you can't afford, go where cars go to die." Past the cement silos Dai finds Fumie Tanabe on a forklift and, in the weeds behind a stack of crushed Corollas, a 1989 Roadster in Mariner Blue faded to chalk: rear end crumpled, roof torn, a cat living on the passenger seat. She asks 150,000. He has 41,000. The haggling is a comedy of numbers and insults and ends at 60,000: 20,000 down, the rest in Sunday labor at 3,000 a day plus cash by Christmas, and the car stays in her yard and her name until it's paid (the last owner dumped it with its papers; plates and shaken run until March 1996). On his first Sunday he strips a Corolla and slices his hand; she bandages it without taking the cigarette out of her mouth. At the back of the yard a long shape sits under a grey tarp: "Not for sale. Don't touch that one." Ends with Dai in the driver's seat of a car that doesn't run, hands at nine and three, the cat on his knees. The car mirrors hers in model and nothing else; the tarp is planted.

**Ch5. Interview** (18-26 Sep; manga ch9). Aya cuts his hair at the kitchen table for the interview, fast and exact, and lets slip that a salon would charge 3,000 yen for it (her dream, planted). She sees the scar under his fringe and says nothing kind, which is how she is kind. Michiko irons his collar at 5 a.m. before her factory shift. At Sanko Brake the personnel manager asks about the gap in his junior-high record. Dai feels his temper come up, sits on it, and answers in one plain sentence. He has no idea if he passed. Monday night, 25 Sep: Michiko is on overnight stocktaking at the supermarket. At 1 a.m. Sota's breathing goes high and thin and the blue inhaler stops working. Aya freezes. Dai calls 119 from the kitchen phone and rides in the ambulance with Sota's hand in his. Ends in the emergency room when the curtain is pulled back by a nurse with her hair pinned up: Natsume. Dai holds it together for others; his two worlds collide.

**Ch6. Night Shift** (26-28 Sep; manga ch10). At work Natsume is someone else: quick hands, a soft voice for Sota ("breathe out like you're blowing out candles"), nothing at all for Dai. Michiko arrives running in her supermarket vest and pays at the night window, partly in coins. Natsume sees. At 5 a.m. at the vending machine she buys a can of corn soup and finally talks to him: "Your mother works two jobs. Your brother can't breathe. And you want that road." "You drive it." "Nobody waits up for me." She mentions that a home nebulizer would help Sota. On the bench at dawn Michiko thanks Dai and lets slip that his father, whenever the kids were sick, went out to play pachinko because he couldn't stand waiting rooms. Thursday: Natsume asks after Sota at the pump, then talks with Kubo by the air pump where Dai can't hear (Kubo tells her about Pier 3 and the scrapyard). Afterwards Kubo says: "Sunday. Six a.m. Tanabe's. She says bring gloves." She agrees only after seeing his family; Kubo chooses to help.

**Ch7. Clutch** (1-7 Oct; manga ch11). Fumie and Natsume know each other: "Two years, girl." Fumie calls her Akane-chan, and that is how Dai learns her first name. In the back lot, walled in by stacked wrecks, Akane puts him in the driver's seat of the red Roadster and makes him set the seat, the mirrors and his hands, and tell her what her feet did that night. Then he stalls it eleven times. Good eyes do not make good feet. Fumie keeps count from the forklift (she had bet herself nine). Akane is merciless and exact; on the twelfth try he pulls away clean and takes a lap of the yard in second gear without breathing. Her rules: private ground until he has a license, never the bypass, and "Lie to me once and we're done." He promises. Ends with Fumie, after Akane has gone: "That girl hasn't set foot in my yard since they brought in the..." She stops. "Get back to work." No instant prodigy, and a promise on the wall like a gun.

**Ch8. Timing Belt** (8-18 Oct; manga ch12). Kubo comes to the yard on a Sunday afternoon with a toolbox older than Dai and listens to the dead engine the way he listened to the sedan in manga ch2. The verdict: a snapped timing belt, and this engine doesn't wreck its valves when that happens. The car was never dead, just abandoned by someone who didn't know. Kubo's hands give him away and he admits it: twelve years with his own garage, until the bubble burst. Okabe sells Dai four used tires and remembers him: "The kid who watches money." Mr. Hotta warns that sleeping through class could cost him graduation and the job. Aya smells gasoline on him and says nothing; a bar of heavy-duty hand soap appears by the sink. On 18 Oct the letter comes: Sanko Brake offers him a job from 1 April. Michiko reads it twice, cries at the stove with her back to the room, and pins it to the fridge with a magnet. Ends with Dai at 4 a.m. looking at the letter. A real future against the dream; Kubo becomes a mentor with a past.

**Ch9. Type R** (19-27 Oct; manga ch13). The warm chapter. Thursday nights become short talks at the pump: Akane doesn't eat breakfast, reads paperbacks in the car, and says his cap makes him look twelve. Sunday lessons move on to heel-and-toe on the yard straight. Sota's Mini 4WD race at the hobby shop goes badly; Dai, who knows nothing about toy cars, sees that Sota's car flies off at the same corner every time and helps him move the weight, and they laugh because the thing can't even steer. Nobu finally sees the yard (Dai needs help carrying a used soft top), swears on his camera to keep the secret, and feeds Dai free ramen at his father's shop. Aya, on the payphone outside the danchi, giggles; Dai has never heard her giggle. At 4 a.m. Friday a brand-new white Integra Type R pulls in. The driver, polite and curious, pays with a 10,000-yen note and asks if a red Roadster fills up here. Dai says no. The man reads his name tag: "You're a bad liar, Hayama-kun." Everyday warmth before the costs; the rival planted.

**Ch10. Side Bet** (27-29 Oct; manga ch14). Michiko asks Dai for money. Sota's ER bill and a new daily inhaler have broken the month, and she has never asked anyone for anything. He gives her the cabinet money and more, and then can't make Fumie's payment or buy brake pads. On Saturday he goes back to Pier 3 alone, reads the favorite's corded tires and shaking hands, and puts 10,000 yen against him. He wins 18,000 after Okabe's cut. Okabe: "You've got an eye. Ever bet on a sure thing? Yourself?" The memory Dai keeps away comes up anyway: pachinko balls spilled in the genkan, and men at the door when he was nine, asking for his father. He pays Fumie, buys the pads, tells nobody. Ends at dawn on the overpass, counting money he didn't earn by the hour. First taste of betting, the father's shadow, and a choice for family first (which makes his later failure hurt).

**Ch11. The Other Tarp** (29 Oct-3 Nov; manga ch15). His car is on its wheels. Sent to fetch a jack from the back of the yard, Dai lifts the forbidden tarp: a black Toyota MR2, its right side folded in, the windshield starred, a faded traffic-safety charm from Shiomi Hachiman shrine on the mirror, and a Momo steering wheel. Akane sees him there. She leaves without a word; the lesson is over. Fumie: "Not my story." On Thursday Akane doesn't come to the station. At 6 a.m. on Friday 3 Nov (a holiday, and, as Fumie let slip, Akane's 22nd birthday) Dai walks to the overpass and finds the red Roadster parked at the foot of the stairs and Akane standing by the new guardrail. He buys two cans of corn soup and hands her one: "Happy birthday." She almost laughs. Then: "Two years ago a friend of mine went into that pillar. I was in front of him." He doesn't ask anything else, which is new for him. Ends with her: "Get your car running. I'll teach you to drive it. In the yard. You promised, kid." "I know." The mystery opens a crack; the promise gets its weight.

**Ch12. Ignition** (4-14 Nov; manga ch16-17). The last parts go in: taillights from a wreck, the used top, a new battery. Sunday 5 Nov, with Kubo, Fumie, Nobu and Sota (sworn to secrecy, hopping from foot to foot) watching, Dai turns the key: a click (loose terminal), a cough, then the engine catches, lifters ticking, idle hunting and then settling. He drives Sota round the yard at 20 km/h and Sota laughs without coughing. All week he keeps his promise; on Wednesday night he sits in the yard with the engine running and the gate chained. Then, at 3 a.m. on Tuesday 14 Nov, with the gate key Fumie gave him to feed the cat, he unchains the gate and drives out onto the harbor road: headlights up, 40 km/h, terrified and happy. At the red light by the rail crossing a white Silvia pulls up beside him. Goto looks over, looks again, and laughs: "Oi. Bridge kid. Nice car." The light changes, Goto roars off, Dai doesn't follow. He drives back and chains the gate. Ends: "Eighty-seven days to eighteen." He gets what he wanted by breaking his word, and the canon three months begin.
