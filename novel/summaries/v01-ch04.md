# Previously: Volume 1, Chapter 4, "Tanabe Auto Dismantling"

Monday 11 September 1995. Nobu lends Dai a used-car magazine: the cheapest Roadster is a white 1989 with "repair history" at 700,000 yen, "and always add a hundred thousand".

Wednesday, 3 a.m., on the Tuesday shift, Dai does the sum in the margin: 800,000 is 286 of his half-nights, seventy-one and a half weeks, leaving his 41,077 for the license. He'd be nearly nineteen. Kubo reads it upside down: "If you want a car you can't afford, go where cars go to die." Tanabe's, past the silos. "Don't say I sent you. She'll charge you double."

Natsume fills up at 2:20 on Friday morning and glances at the pocket where his hand is running the gears. After the shift (Respect for the Aged Day), Dai flinches at a white Crown taxi and finds Fumie Tanabe on a forklift. In the weeds sits a 1989 Roadster in faded Mariner Blue: white fender, primer hood, rear crushed by a fish truck, roof torn, a half-tailed cat on the passenger seat. Every gear goes in. She asks 150,000; he has 41,077. "Seventy-seven." They settle at 60,000: 20,000 down, seven Sundays of work, and 7,000 / 7,000 / 6,000 at the end of October, November and December. The car stays in her yard and her name. "The extra thousand's for the cat."

Saturday, Mom drills him with Hotta's sixteen interview questions and slides her finger past number ten. Sota finally gets his moon: "Thank you for looking."

Sunday, Dai pays and strips a Corolla. Fumie shares her lunch, peels one of Mom's pears and tells him about Miyazaki in 1958. He slices his left palm on a window-motor hole; she bandages it and lets his tetanus lie go. Sweeping, he lifts a grey tarp at the back: "Not for sale. Don't touch that one." He ends in the Roadster's seat, hands at nine and three, the cat on his knees.
