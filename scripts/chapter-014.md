CAPTION: "OUR GENKAN. THE SAME CONCRETE AS NOW. MY FATHER SITTING ON THE STEP AT NIGHT TO TAKE HIS SHOES OFF, LAUGHING AT SOMETHING," CAPTION: "AND WHEN HE STOOD UP, SILVER BALLS CAME OUT OF THE TURN-UPS OF HIS TROUSERS AND RAN INTO THE CORNERS BY THE DOOR."
- P2 (middle-right): Memory: a small thin boy with long messy hair and a girl with a bob on hands and knees on the concrete, reaching into the corners after the rolling balls; the man's trouser legs and socks at the edge. CAPTION: "AYA AND I WENT AFTER THEM ON OUR HANDS AND KNEES."
- P3 (middle-left): Memory close: a small boy's palm holding three shiny silver pachinko balls. CAPTION: "THEY WERE HEAVIER THAN THEY LOOKED, AND COLD. HE'D LAUGH MORE WHEN WE FOUND THEM."
- P4 (bottom, wide): Memory: under the kitchen sink, a cupboard door open, a glass jam jar half full of silver balls; a small boy's hand dropping one more in. CAPTION: "I KEPT THEM IN A JAM JAR UNDER THE SINK, AND BY THE TIME I WAS EIGHT THERE WERE ENOUGH TO RATTLE." SFX: "karan"

## Page 44: Good shoes
- P1 (top, wide): Memory: the steel door open only as far as its chain; through the gap two men in dark well-cut suits on the landing, polite; inside, a younger tired woman (hair still black, low bun) holding the door, and behind her the thin nine-year-old boy with long messy hair peering round the kitchen frame. CAPTION: "WHEN I WAS NINE THERE WERE TWO MEN AT THAT DOOR, IN GOOD SHOES. THEY WERE VERY POLITE. THEY ASKED FOR HIM BY HIS WHOLE NAME."
- P2 (middle-right): Memory close, low: two pairs of very polished black shoes on the concrete landing, seen through the chained gap of the door. CAPTION: "MOM KEPT THE CHAIN ON AND SAID HE WASN'T HERE. ONE OF THEM SAID THAT WAS ALL RIGHT, THEY'D COME AGAIN." CAPTION: "THEY DID."
- P3 (middle-left): Memory: the cupboard under the kitchen sink, open and empty, dark. Silent. CAPTION: "I DON'T KNOW WHAT HAPPENED TO THE JAR."
- P4 (lower, wide): Present, night, no moon: Dai small crossing a single freight rail track on a low hump of the harbor road under the lone crossing light; off to one side a dark lane ending at a pale fence. CAPTION: "I WENT OVER THE RAILS AND PAST THE END OF TANABE-SAN'S LANE, WHICH WAS DARK, THE FENCE AT THE BOTTOM JUST A PALER DARK."
- P5 (bottom, wide): Night, between tall dark grain silos: close on Dai's taped sneaker stepping on a shard of black plastic on the dark road. CAPTION: "BETWEEN THE SILOS SOMETHING CRUNCHED UNDER MY SHOE. PLASTIC, OFF THE CORNER OF HIS BUMPER, I THOUGHT." SFX: "paki"

## Page 45: Rubber in the cold
- P1 (top, wide): Dai standing alone in the dark S-bend between the towering silos, a faint haze hanging in the air under one lamp. CAPTION: "TOO DARK TO SEE THE ROAD, BUT I COULD SMELL IT, RUBBER, STILL HANGING THERE IN THE COLD. I STOOD IN IT FOR A WHILE."
- P2 (middle-right): Close on Dai's face in the dark, unsure. CAPTION: "IT DIDN'T TELL ME ANYTHING. IT COULD HAVE BEEN THE BACK TIRE. OR HIS FOOT, OR DIESEL OFF A FISH TRUCK, OR THE STARLET'S LIGHTS IN HIS MIRROR."
- P3 (middle-left): Dai looking down at his own feet on the dark asphalt. CAPTION: "I'D ONLY SEEN THE FRONT."
- P4 (lower, wide): Dai walking along the empty footpath beside the deserted elevated bypass under orange lamps. CAPTION: "UP ONTO THE FOOTPATH ALONG THE BYPASS, WHERE THE TRUCKS GO BY AT YOUR ELBOW IN THE DAYTIME AND NOTHING WENT BY NOW."
- P5 (bottom, wide): The empty pedestrian overpass over the bypass curve at night, nobody on it. CAPTION: "THE OVERPASS WAS EMPTY."

## Page 46: Where I stand
- P1 (top, wide): Close on the overpass deck by the railing: eight or nine cigarette ends and a scatter of ash. CAPTION: "BY THE RAILING OVER THE MIDDLE OF THE CURVE THERE WERE CIGARETTE ENDS, EIGHT OR NINE, AND ASH. THE LOOKOUT'S. HE'D BEEN STANDING WHERE I STAND."
- P2 (middle-right): Dai sitting on the walkway with his back against the tall railing, knees up, jacket zipped, one hand in the jacket pocket and the other in his jeans pocket. CAPTION: "IT WAS NEARLY THREE. I SAT WITH MY BACK AGAINST THE RAILING, ONE HAND ON THE GLOVES AND THE OTHER ON THE MONEY."
- P3 (middle-left): Silent, far: the overpass over the empty lamp-lit road, Dai a tiny curled shape asleep against the railing. CAPTION: "I DON'T KNOW WHEN I WENT TO SLEEP."
- P4 (bottom, large): Dawn: Dai waking, hunched and shivering against the railing; the lamp at the top of the stairs still on; behind the harbor cranes the sky pale along the bottom. CAPTION: "WHEN I WOKE UP I WAS COLD ALL THE WAY THROUGH, AND IT WAS HALF PAST FIVE. BEHIND THE CRANES THE SKY HAD STOPPED BEING BLACK ALONG THE BOTTOM."

## Page 47: Into hours
- P1 (top-right): Close under the lamp: Dai's hands holding two 10,000-yen notes, one 5,000-yen note, three 1,000-yen notes and a few coins. CAPTION: "TWO 10,000S. A 5,000. THREE 1,000S. 97 YEN IN COINS."
- P2 (top-left): Dai folding the 5,000 and two 1,000s together small and buttoning them into his shirt pocket. CAPTION: "THE 5,000 AND TWO 1,000S, FOLDED SMALL, INTO MY SHIRT POCKET. TANABE-SAN'S. KUBO-SAN'S SIX WOULD COME OUT OF A 10,000."
- P3 (middle, wide): Dai standing at the railing in the grey light, looking down the curve, working it out. CAPTION: "EIGHTEEN THOUSAND. TWO THOUSAND MORE THAN THE BOY WHO'D DRIVEN. I DID IT IN HOURS, BECAUSE THAT'S WHAT I DO." CAPTION: "FIFTY-ONE HOURS AND TWENTY-SIX MINUTES AT THE PUMPS. SIX NIGHTS, AND A PIECE OF A SEVENTH. I'D STOOD ON A BOLLARD FOR ABOUT FOUR MINUTES."
- P4 (lower, large): BIG wide dawn view from above: the first car of the morning, an ordinary small sedan with its headlights still on, coming round the long left-hand curve of the empty four-lane bypass, not fast, about to pass under the overpass; Dai small at the railing above; pale sky over the factories. CAPTION: "THE FIRST CAR OF THE MORNING CAME ROUND THE LEFT-HANDER WITH ITS HEADLIGHTS STILL ON, NOT FAST, AND WENT UNDER MY FEET."
- P5 (bottom, wide): Close on Dai's hand pushing the rest of the folded notes into his jeans pocket; the jacket pocket with the gloves above it. CAPTION: "I PUT THE REST BACK IN MY TROUSER POCKET. NOT IN THE JACKET, WITH THE GLOVES AND SOTA'S LIST AND THE TOOTH."
